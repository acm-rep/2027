We solicit papers describing original work relevant to reproducibility and independent verification of scientific results. The submission must not be published or under review elsewhere. ACM REP 2027 is a **double-blind** reviewed conference. Author names should not be included. Past work should be referred to in the third person. Self-citations and supplementary material (including code repositories) should also be anonymized.

Submissions will be evaluated according to their significance, originality, technical content, style, clarity, relevance, and likelihood of generating discussion. 

Authors should note that changes to the author list after the submission deadline are not allowed without permission from the PC Chairs. At least one author of each accepted paper is required to register for, attend, and present the work at the conference, either in person or online.

### Long and Short Papers

We solicit both full length papers (10 pages) and short papers (4 pages). The former tend to be descriptions of complete technical work, while the latter tend to be descriptions of interesting, innovative ideas, which nevertheless require more effort to mature. The page limit is without references and/or appendices. Full and short papers will be given a presentation slot in the conference and an entry in the conference proceedings.

### Paper Types

Authors are invited to submit long or short papers in the following four categories:
    
- **Research** papers advance generalizable knowledge through rigorous research. Submissions should include a clearly defined research question, methodology, and supporting evidence.
- **Experience** reports describe practical experiences, approaches, or tools supporting reproducibility, with context, evidence, and lessons learned to inform future practice.
- **Position** papers present arguments, proposals, or critiques concerning reproducibility. Original data is not required.
- **Systematization of knowledge** (SoK) papers critically synthesize and organize existing knowledge about reproducibility. Going beyond a survey of prior work, SoK papers should offer novel insights through taxonomies, conceptual frameworks, critical analyses, or the identification of gaps and future research directions.

### Open Science Policy

**At submission time**: All long and short papers must submit accompanying artifacts, or an explanation of why artifacts are not applicable or cannot be made available. Papers without artifacts or an explanation will be desk-rejected. Artifacts will be made available to the Program Committee for reference, and should be fully anonymized (e.g., via [anonymous 4open science](https://anonymous.4open.science/)). Authors may include in their submission an Artifact Description Appendix up to one page long.

**For accepted papers**: Artifacts will be reviewed by an Artifact Evaluation Committee following acceptance, and [badges](https://www.acm.org/publications/policies/artifact-review-and-badging-current)  will be awarded per ACM policy. At this stage, all papers must make their accompanying artifacts publicly available in a persistent, long-term repository, meeting at least the requirements for the Artifacts Available badge, or provide an explanation of why this is not applicable or feasible.

### Formatting 

Papers must be submitted in PDF format according to the [ACM template](https://www.acm.org/publications/proceedings-template) published in the ACM guidelines, selecting the generic “sigconf” sample. The PDF files must have all non-standard fonts embedded. Papers must be self-contained and in English. If submitting a short paper, authors must indicate “SHORT:” at the beginning of the title. The review process is double-blind. 

### Policy on AI Use by Authors and Reviewers 

ACM REP follows the [ACM Policy on Authorship](https://www.acm.org/publications/policies/new-acm-policy-on-authorship) and the [ACM Peer Review Policy](https://www.acm.org/publications/policies/peer-review-faq) regarding the use of generative AI and large language models (LLMs).

**Authors**: The use of AI tools to assist with writing is permitted and does not require disclosure. However, the use of AI tools in conducting research, including research design, data generation, implementation, analysis, and the creation of research artifacts, must be disclosed in an AI Disclosure Appendix. Authors remain fully responsible for the accuracy, originality, and integrity of their submissions, including AI-generated content, code, and references.

{{< callout note >}}
Submissions must demonstrate substantive scientific or scholarly contributions, regardless of whether AI tools were used in their preparation. Papers that lack meaningful original contributions, rigorous analysis, or sufficient technical substance, including submissions consisting primarily of superficial or uncritically generated AI content, may be desk-rejected by the Program Chairs without review by the Program Committee.
{{< /callout >}}

**Reviewers**: Reviewers must maintain the confidentiality of submissions and must not share manuscript content with AI tools or third-party services that do not guarantee confidentiality. AI tools must not substitute for independent evaluation and judgment. Their use for improving the grammar, clarity, and presentation of reviewer-written text is permitted, provided confidentiality is preserved. Reviewers remain fully responsible for their reviews.
